# Quantum × HPC Pathways: Verified Data for a South Side Chicago Quantum/HPC Workforce Initiative

## TL;DR
- **The opportunity is real but the near-term job base is small and skewed to degree-holders**: IQMP's legally-binding tax-incentive job commitments total only ~300 (PsiQuantum 154, Pasqal 50, Infleqtion 36 new + 14 retained, IBM 50, Diraq 2–4), and ~95% require a degree — but a 2024 Boston Consulting Group analysis for the Chicago Quantum Exchange projects up to **191,000 quantum jobs** in the Illinois-Wisconsin-Indiana region by 2035, with more than 70% (≈136,000) open to workers without graduate degrees and ~32% open to those with associate's degrees or technical training. This gap between today's ~300 degree-heavy jobs and a large, conditional 2035 upside is the core case for a community-college-anchored pathway.
- **A credible credential-and-employer spine already exists**: IBM's new FutureNow Chicago hub (750 jobs) plus a City Colleges of Chicago apprenticeship (500 apprentices over 5 years; IBM to hire one-third of qualified graduates), free IBM SkillsBuild quantum credentials, CQE's free Q-Ready program and Talent Portal (755 jobs/62 employers), and Argonne/Fermilab technician-and-community-college pipelines (DOE CCI, Fermilab TECHS and PACMAN).
- **Wraparound "Vulnerability-axis" supports are well-established and citable**: Illinois CCAP (childcare for adults in training, administered in Cook County by Illinois Action for Children), RTA/Ventra reduced-fare programs, WIOA American Job Centers (including Mid-South at 4314 S. Cottage Grove), One Million Degrees (up to $1,000 stipends + coaching, with documented long-run earnings gains), and CCC's own WEI/Project Evolve programs bundling free tuition, transit cards, and childcare funds.

## Key Findings

### Opportunity Axis — Credentials & Training
1. **City Colleges of Chicago (CCC) IBM apprenticeship** — new program supporting 500 apprentices over five years; IBM commits to hiring one-third of qualified graduates; year-long, paid; funded via philanthropy (goal of $7M for the first 2–3 years via the Chicagoland Workforce Funder Alliance). Anchored at Olive-Harvey College (South Side). Primary sources: CCC newsroom, Chicago Sun-Times, Capitol News Illinois, Crain's (all April 2026).
2. **IBM SkillsBuild** — free online platform with quantum-computing digital credentials (foundational quantum computing credential; "Quantum Enigmas") awarded via Credly; open to adult learners 18+. Primary source: skillsbuild.org, credly.com/org/ibm.
3. **Chicago Quantum Exchange (CQE) pipelines** — Q-Ready (free professional development, launched March 2026, ~500 participants); CQE Talent Portal (755 jobs, 62 employers, funded by Brinson Foundation); Open Quantum Initiative (OQI) Undergraduate Fellowship (10-week paid summer research); online Course in Quantum Science, Networking, and Communications (8 weeks). Primary source: chicagoquantum.org.
4. **ISTC quantum talent framework** — Illinois produced 33,441 quantum-relevant postsecondary completions in 2024 (up 33% since 2018), spanning certificates through PhDs, using 171 quantum-relevant CIP codes; certificate programs were the single largest category. Primary source: ISTC report "Mapping Illinois' Quantum Talent Pipeline" (May 21, 2026).
5. **IQMP construction trades pipeline** — HIRE360 recruits candidates into union trades apprenticeships for IQMP construction (with Related Midwest and Clayco). Primary source: hire360chicago.com/iqmp.

### Opportunity Axis — Employer Demand
- **PsiQuantum** anchors IQMP, building the first US utility-scale/fault-tolerant quantum computer at the former South Works site; broke ground September 2025; hiring in Chicago (roles ~$107K–$147K). Incentive commitment: 154 jobs; state funding $92M (~$600,000/job). Primary: psiquantum.com/illinois, Illinois Answers.
- **Infleqtion** headquartering global quantum computing operations in Illinois; ~$50M investment; the MICRO tax-credit agreement corresponds to a $14M investment for a tax credit estimated at $5.3M and 50 new roles across engineering, commercialization, and research; plans first neutral-atom quantum computer at IQMP by 2027 and a downtown Chicago Quantum Innovation Center. Incentive commitment: 36 new + 14 retained jobs. Primary: Illinois EDC/IQMP press releases, Quantum Computing Report, Forbes (July 2026).
- **IBM FutureNow Chicago** — 750 new full-time jobs in AI, cybersecurity, data science, and quantum; anchors the "Quantum Works" building expected to open in 2028. Primary: gov-pritzker-newsroom, CCC, Crain's.
- **Other IQMP tenants**: Pasqal (50 jobs), Diraq (2–4 jobs).
- **National labs**: Argonne (Q-NEXT DOE center; DOE Community College Internship program; Bridge Into Internships for South Side high schoolers; SULI); Fermilab (TECHS high-school technician apprenticeship funded $2M/5yr; PACMAN apprenticeship with College of DuPage; SQMS center; paid year-long quantum computing internship). Primary: anl.gov, fnal.gov.
- **Labor market data**: O*NET/BLS "Network and Computer Systems Administrators" (15-1244.00); overall computer/IT occupations projected to grow much faster than average 2024–2034, ~317,700 openings/yr, median wage $105,990 (May 2024). No dedicated SOC/O*NET code yet exists for "quantum technician." Primary: bls.gov, onetonline.org.
- **Regional projection**: BCG for CQE (2024) — up to 191,000 quantum jobs in IL-WI-IN by 2035; more than 70% (≈136,000) open to those without graduate degrees; ~32% to associate's/technical training; up to $80B economic impact by 2035. Growth projected >200% (2027–2030), then >550% (2030–2035). Explicitly conditional on continued government investment. Primary: chicagoquantum.org/jobprojectiondata.

### Opportunity Axis — Financial Support
- **CCC Workforce Empowerment Initiative (WEI)** — tuition-free short programs (Project Evolve at Olive-Harvey; Project MPACT at Malcolm X; Technology Works at Daley) with free tuition, transportation cards, childcare funds, and stipends up to $500. Funded by the Illinois Community College Board WEI grant ($5.5M).
- **Future Ready CCC** — last-dollar scholarship covering the tuition gap; **Fresh Start** debt relief.
- **WIOA Individual Training Accounts** via Chicago Cook Workforce Partnership (LWIA 7) American Job Centers — training funds for eligible adults.
- **Illinois Works Pre-Apprenticeship Program** — DCEO, $18M / 1,700 participants (construction trades; relevant to IQMP build-out).
- **State/federal quantum investment context**: >$200M state + ~$300M federal awarded to date; proposed $500M state budget line for quantum/microelectronics.

### Vulnerability Axis — Structured Supports
- **Childcare**: Illinois Child Care Assistance Program (CCAP) explicitly covers parents "attending education or training activities including … vocational training and 2- and 4-year college degree programs." Administered in Cook County by Illinois Action for Children (South Side/Chatham office, 8741 S. Greenwood Ave; 312.823.1100). CCC operates Child Development Laboratory Schools at five campuses.
- **Transportation**: RTA reduced-fare and ride-free programs via Ventra; RTA Transit Benefit (pre-tax, up to $300/month, ~40% savings). Note: RTA ride-free is largely limited to seniors/disabled/Benefit Access enrollees — general low-income trainees rely on reduced fare and program-provided transit cards (e.g., via CCC WEI).
- **Financial-aid navigation / benefits counseling**: One Million Degrees (wraparound coaching, up to $1,000/yr performance stipends, childcare/transportation navigation; expanding to 3,000 CCC students/yr; up to $5M/yr CCC investment); CCC Financial Aid Offices and Wellness Centers; Chicago Cook Workforce Partnership American Job Centers (Mid-South, 4314 S. Cottage Grove).

## Details

### 1. OPPORTUNITY AXIS — Credential and Training Programs

**City Colleges of Chicago + IBM apprenticeship (flagship pathway).** On April 29, 2026, Governor Pritzker and IBM announced IBM's FutureNow Chicago delivery center at IQMP, creating 750 full-time jobs in AI, cybersecurity, data science, and quantum. As part of it, CCC is launching a new apprenticeship program that will support 500 apprentices over five years, with IBM committing to hire one-third of qualified graduates. The announcement was made at Olive-Harvey College, one of seven City Colleges. CCC Chancellor Juan Salgado called it "a once-in-a-generation opportunity." Apprentices are to be compensated directly for a year-long program; funds come from philanthropy, with Chicagoland Workforce Funder Alliance executive director Matthew Bruce citing a goal of raising $7 million for the first two-to-three years. IBM and FutureNow Chicago will anchor the park's "Quantum Works" building, expected to open in 2028. *Sources: CCC newsroom (colleges.ccc.edu, primary, current); Chicago Sun-Times; Capitol News Illinois; Crain's Chicago Business — all April 2026, current.*

**Pilot quantum-engineering program (CCC + UChicago PME + Quantum Machines).** Seven CCC students/alumni completed a spring pilot in quantum engineering ("quantum control") through the University of Chicago Pritzker School of Molecular Engineering with Quantum Machines, organized by CQE. *Source: pme.uchicago.edu (primary). Small pilot — flag as early-stage, not a standing credential.*

**IBM SkillsBuild.** Free platform; digital credentials in quantum computing including a foundational quantum computing credential (superposition, entanglement, measurement; IBM Quantum Composer) and "Quantum Enigmas" (requires passing module quizzes at 80%+; open to registered adult learners 18+); badges issued via Credly. IBM Quantum Learning additionally offers a free university-level series, "Understanding quantum information and computation" (John Watrous, 16 lessons), and open-source Qiskit. *Sources: skillsbuild.org/students/digital-credentials, credly.com/org/ibm, ibm.com/quantum (all primary, current).*

**Chicago Quantum Exchange pipelines.** Q-Ready (free, launched March 2026, ~500 participants; open to undergrads/grads/postdocs in IL/WI/IN; virtual with one in-person Quantum Recruiting Forum). CQE Talent Portal (launched with 755 jobs across 62 employers including Allstate, BMO, KPMG; funded by the Brinson Foundation). OQI Undergraduate Fellowship (10-week paid summer research at UChicago, Argonne, Fermilab, UIUC, Wisconsin, Northwestern, Ohio State, Purdue; 27 fellows in 2025). Online 8-week Course in Quantum Science, Networking, and Communications aimed at reskilling working scientists/engineers. Emily Easton is CQE Director of Education and Workforce Development and IQMP Workforce Development Co-Lead. *Source: chicagoquantum.org (primary, current). Note: OQI and Q-Ready currently target students/postdocs, not adult career-changers without a degree — a gap for a community pathway.*

**ISTC quantum talent framework.** "Mapping Illinois' Quantum Talent Pipeline" (ISTC with Illinois EDC, IQMP, CQE; released May 21, 2026) established 171 quantum-relevant CIP codes and found Illinois produced 33,441 quantum-relevant postsecondary completions in 2024 (up 33% since the 2018 National Quantum Initiative Act; up 60% over the decade). Illinois accounted for nearly 5% of all quantum-relevant program completions nationally; certificate programs were the single largest category of completions statewide, and nearly 3,000 computer-science master's degrees were awarded in 2024 alone. The report emphasizes certificate and community-college pathways for workers without advanced degrees. *Sources: istcoalition.org, illinoisedc.org, iqmp.org (primary, current).*

**IQMP construction trades.** HIRE360 (with Related Midwest and Clayco) recruits into union trades apprenticeships for IQMP construction. *Source: hire360chicago.com/iqmp (primary).*

### 2. OPPORTUNITY AXIS — Employer Demand

**IQMP job commitments (Illinois Answers Project, Feb 5, 2026, citing DCEO incentive agreements + BLS).** Legally-binding new-job requirements total 240 (PsiQuantum 154, Pasqal 50, Infleqtion 36 new + 14 retained); plus IBM 50 and Diraq 2–4 announced — ~300 known IQMP jobs. The state can currently "guarantee 254 new jobs." ~95% of the jobs require a degree; ~18% require an advanced degree; >72% are in engineering, computer science, math, or physics. Incentives per job are among the highest in the state: the median Illinois incentive is $20,000/job, but "IQMP-related agreements average $276,000 dollars per job created or retained, more than 95% of similar agreements in other industries. The only agreements that outpace quantum tech tax breaks are tied to data center projects." By company: PsiQuantum $92M/150 jobs (~$600,000/job), Pasqal ~$122,000/job, Infleqtion ~$107,000/job. "Industry experts say quantum companies are still five-to-10 years away from profitability, meaning current hiring is focused on research, development and scaling, not mass employment." *Source: illinoisanswers.org (nonprofit investigative journalism; primary data from DCEO/BLS; current). This is the single best "reality check" source — flag that boosters' "thousands of jobs" claims are aspirational versus the ~300 contractually required.*

**PsiQuantum.** Building America's first utility-scale, fault-tolerant (million-qubit-scale) quantum computer at IQMP (former U.S. Steel South Works); broke ground September 2025; silicon-photonics architecture (partner GlobalFoundries). Chicago on-site roles posted (~$107K–$147K). *Source: psiquantum.com/illinois (primary, current).*

**Infleqtion.** Headquartering global quantum computing operations in Illinois (~$50M investment); the MICRO tax-credit agreement corresponds to a $14M investment for a tax credit estimated at $5.3 million and 50 new roles across engineering, commercialization, and research; the July 24, 2025 announcement targets 100 logical qubits using thousands of neutral-atom qubits on the Sqale platform; plans first neutral-atom quantum computer at IQMP by 2027 and a downtown Chicago Quantum Innovation Center; a 50-logical-qubit system was announced July 2026. Advertised roles include Quantum Physicist ($115K–$165K) and Quantum Software Engineer. *Sources: Illinois EDC/IQMP press releases (primary); Quantum Computing Report; Forbes (July 22, 2026); ZipRecruiter/BuiltIn (job listings — secondary, use only for role/salary color).*

**National labs.**
- *Argonne*: Q-NEXT (DOE National QIS Research Center, renewed five years) trains "scientists, engineers, technicians and other professionals"; DOE Community College Internship (CCI) program (10-week paid; Fall 2026 deadline May 20, 2026); "Bridge Into Internships" partners with South Side high schools; SULI undergraduate program; OQI host. *Source: anl.gov (primary, current).*
- *Fermilab*: SQMS quantum center; "Technician Education for Careers out of High School" (TECHS) apprenticeship pilot ($2M over five years from DOE Office of High Energy Physics); Particle Accelerator Capabilities Modernization Apprenticeship (PACMAN) with College of DuPage (electronics technicians); Quantum Computing Internship for Physics Undergraduates (paid, year-long); VALOR apprenticeships. *Source: fnal.gov (primary, current). These are the strongest examples of sub-baccalaureate, technician-level lab pipelines relevant to a community program.*

**Labor market data.** No dedicated federal SOC/O*NET code exists for "quantum technician" — confirmed by ISTC's need to build a custom CIP framework. Closest codes: Network and Computer Systems Administrators (O*NET/SOC 15-1244.00; declining employment but ~14,300 openings/yr 2024–2034, mostly replacement); Computer Systems Analysts (~34,200 openings/yr). Overall computer/IT occupations are projected to grow "much faster than average" 2024–2034 with ~317,700 openings/yr and a $105,990 median wage (May 2024). *Sources: bls.gov Occupational Outlook Handbook, onetonline.org (government/primary, current). Flag: use IT-adjacent codes as proxies and note the absence of a quantum-specific occupation code.*

**Regional projection (BCG for CQE, 2024).** Up to 191,000 quantum-technology jobs in IL-WI-IN by 2035; more than 70% (≈136,000) open to people without graduate degrees; ~32% open to workers with associate's degrees or technical training; up to $80 billion economic impact by 2035. Growth projected more than 200% from 2027 to 2030, then more than 550% between 2030 and 2035 — explicitly conditional on continued government investment. A separate CQE analysis of more than 5,000 real job postings from QED-C and Quantum Computing Report (2021–2023) found the share requiring a bachelor's degree or less was 52% (2021), 56% (2022), and 55% (2023), while the PhD-requiring share slipped from about 35% (2021) to 29% (2023). *Sources: chicagoquantum.org/jobprojectiondata, /degreereports, /economicimpactdata (CQE primary data pages, 2024); corroborated by Argonne, UChicago PME, HPCwire, Physics Today. Flag: a "60% are not PhDs / ~200K jobs" phrasing appears only in Forbes (July 2026) as informal industry shorthand — use CQE's authoritative >70%/136,000 figures instead.*

### 3. OPPORTUNITY AXIS — Financial Support

**Future Ready CCC** — last-dollar scholarship; **Fresh Start** debt relief. *Source: pages.ccc.edu (primary).*

**WIOA / Chicago Cook Workforce Partnership (LWIA 7).** Individual Training Accounts fund approved training for eligible adults (priority to public-assistance recipients, low-income, and basic-skills-deficient individuals). ~10 American Job Centers; the South Side Mid-South center is at 4314 S. Cottage Grove. *Sources: dol.gov, ccc.edu WIOA pages, chicookworks.org (government/primary, current).*

**Illinois Works Pre-Apprenticeship** — DCEO, $18M to 36 recipients serving 1,700 (construction trades; relevant to IQMP build-out). *Source: dceo.illinois.gov (primary).*

**Quantum funding context** — >$200M state + ~$300M federal to date; proposed $500M state budget line for quantum/microelectronics. *Source: innovate-illinois.com DCEO (primary).*

### 4. VULNERABILITY AXIS — Structured Supports

**Childcare.** Illinois CCAP (IDHS-funded) helps low-income families pay for care for children under 13 while the parent is "working, OR looking for work or school, OR … attending education or training activities including ESL, high school equivalence, GED, vocational training, and 2- and 4-year college degree programs." Cook County administrator: Illinois Action for Children (South Side/Chatham office at 8741 S. Greenwood Ave, Suite 300; main line 312.823.1100). CCC operates Child Development Laboratory Schools (ages 2–5) at five campuses. Federal CCAMPIS grants support campus childcare for student-parents (e.g., UIC's Little Sparks). *Sources: dhs.state.il.us, actforchildren.org, ccc.edu (government/primary, current — CCAP policy manual updated June 2026).*

**Transportation.** RTA reduced-fare and ride-free programs administered via Ventra; RTA Transit Benefit Fare Program (pre-tax, up to $300/month, ~40% savings). **Important limitation**: RTA ride-free is largely restricted to seniors, people with disabilities, and Illinois Department on Aging Benefit Access enrollees; general low-income adult trainees are not automatically eligible for free rides and rely on reduced-fare permits or program-issued transit cards. A Chicago Jobs Council survey previously documented that Ventra's structure reduced the transit assistance job-seeker agencies could provide. Practical workaround: CCC WEI/Project Evolve provide transportation cards directly. *Sources: rtachicago.org, pacebus.com, ventrachicago.com (primary); CBS Chicago/Chicago Jobs Council (secondary, older — flag as dated context).*

**Financial-aid navigation / benefits counseling.** One Million Degrees (OMD) — Chicago nonprofit (founded 2006) providing wraparound coaching for low-income community-college students across four pillars (academic, financial, professional, personal); up to $1,000/year performance-based stipends usable for books/transportation; coordinators (≈65 students each) connect students to childcare and other supports. April 2026: CCC and OMD announced a five-year expansion to reach 3,000 students/year, starting at Olive-Harvey College (South Side), with up to $5M/year CCC investment plus $5M commitments each from the CCC Foundation and the Pritzker Foundation. Evidence base: a University of Chicago Inclusive Economy Lab randomized study (2021, led by Pritzker Director Marianne Bertrand) found OMD enrollees were 18% more likely to earn a degree within three years, and those who applied as high-school seniors were 73% more likely to graduate in three years; the latest findings show OMD increased annual earnings by more than $14,000 on average seven years after enrollment. A December 2, 2025 multi-year CCC evaluation found participants earned 3.2× more credits than the control group and were 2.6× more likely to persist from Fall to Spring. CCC Financial Aid Offices and Wellness Centers (mental-health counseling, food pantries, community-resource referrals) provide additional navigation, and the Greater Chicago Food Depository "Food Security for Life" partnership addresses food insecurity. *Sources: onemilliondegrees.org, colleges.ccc.edu, harris.uchicago.edu, civicengagement.uchicago.edu, WBEZ/Sun-Times/Chalkbeat/Higher Ed Dive (primary + reputable news, current — April 2026).*

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Build the tool's data spine on contractually-verified, primary-source facts (now).**
- Populate EMPLOYER_DATA with the five named IQMP employers and their incentive-agreement job counts (PsiQuantum 154, Pasqal 50, Infleqtion 36 new + 14 retained, IBM 50 at IQMP within the 750-job hub, Diraq 2–4), sourced to Illinois Answers/DCEO, and clearly label near-term (contract) versus long-term (191,000 by 2035, BCG/CQE) figures so funders see both the modest guaranteed base and the large conditional upside.
- Populate CREDENTIAL_DATA with: the CCC–IBM apprenticeship, IBM SkillsBuild quantum credentials, CQE Q-Ready/OQI/QSNC course, CCC WEI/Project Evolve, and Fermilab/Argonne technician pipelines — each with a "requires degree?" flag, cost, and duration.
- For every number, store the primary URL and an "as-of" date; prefer CQE data pages, DCEO, BLS, and the ISTC report over news aggregators and job boards.

**Stage 2 — Encode the Vulnerability-axis supports as actionable referrals (now).**
- CCAP (via Illinois Action for Children's South Side office), RTA reduced-fare plus program-issued transit cards, One Million Degrees, and the Mid-South American Job Center (4314 S. Cottage Grove) should each be a structured resource record with eligibility rules, geography, and contact.
- Flag the RTA free-ride eligibility limitation prominently so the tool does not over-promise free transit to general adult trainees.

**Stage 3 — Close the two evidence gaps before funder presentations.**
- Contact CQE (Emily Easton, Education & Workforce Development) for (a) any occupation-level breakdown of the 191,000 projection — the public materials break it out only by education level — and (b) whether Q-Ready/OQI will extend to adult career-changers/community-college students.
- Contact CCC (Olive-Harvey) for the IBM apprenticeship's entry requirements, stipend amount, and whether it grants a stackable credential.

**Benchmarks that would change the recommendation:**
- If IQMP contractual job counts rise materially above ~300, or a quantum-specific SOC/O*NET code is published, upgrade "employer demand" confidence from "conditional/long-horizon" to "current."
- If CCC's apprenticeship publishes sub-baccalaureate entry and a stackable credential, make it the tool's primary recommended pathway; if it requires prior college credit, route users first through WEI/Project Evolve.
- If the proposed $500M state quantum budget is enacted, the BCG 191,000 projection's "continued investment" precondition strengthens — raise the weight given to long-term demand.

## Caveats
- **Near-term vs. aspirational jobs**: The widely-quoted "thousands of jobs" is a booster projection; only ~300 jobs are contractually required today (Illinois Answers/DCEO). The 191,000 regional figure is a 2035 projection explicitly conditioned on continued government investment (BCG for CQE, 2024). Present both, clearly labeled.
- **Degree skew**: ~95% of current IQMP jobs require a degree and >72% are in engineering/CS/math/physics — a genuine tension with a community-access mission. The counterweight is the 2035 projection that >70% of future regional roles won't need a graduate degree, plus CQE posting-analysis showing ~55% of recent quantum postings required a bachelor's or less.
- **No quantum-specific occupation code**: BLS/O*NET have no "quantum technician" SOC code; use IT-adjacent proxies (15-1244.00) and note this explicitly in the tool.
- **Student-vs-adult targeting**: CQE's OQI and Q-Ready currently serve enrolled students/postdocs, and Argonne/Fermilab flagship internships are student-oriented; Fermilab TECHS/PACMAN and CCC WEI are the clearest sub-baccalaureate/adult on-ramps.
- **Dated sources**: The CBS/Chicago Jobs Council Ventra survey is older context; confirm current transit-assistance mechanics before relying on it. Some job-board figures (ZipRecruiter, Zippia) are secondary and fluctuate — use only for salary/role color, not headcounts.
- **Pilot-scale programs**: The CCC–UChicago–Quantum Machines quantum-engineering pilot involved seven students; treat as promising but not yet a standing credential. The Infleqtion "50-logical-qubit"/"100-logical-qubit" figures come from company announcements and reflect targets, not delivered systems.
